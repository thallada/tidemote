# A night's play, 2026-11-05

Left alone with the project for a night, on branch `claude/night-play`. What I built, why, and what the runs said.

## Why these

Reading the design docs, the sharpest critique of the simulation was its own (docs/next-level-2026-10-20.md §2):
*evolution can tune but cannot invent*, and *interactions come in few kinds*: eat, be eaten, bond with kin, shade,
push, warm. Of the six avenues that doc judged strongest, four have been built (phototaxis, catastrophes, the
chronicler, the hand); the two that would let evolution make something new, symbiosis (§4.1) and division of labor
(§4.2), had not been touched. Symbiosis needed no genome growth (the genome's last word was spare), so it was the
one to build.

And watching the page, the first thing I noticed is that the world from afar is a confetti of species colors. The
structure is all there (meadows, fronts, clades, the new species' nurseries) but the eye cannot find it. Microscopists
solved that a century and a half ago: they stain the slide.

## What I built

1. **Grafts** (docs/symbiosis.md): a bond from one species to another, a new gene to make it, energy along it.
   Parasites, passengers and two-colored crusts from one rule, and hosts that escape by evolving out of fit.
2. **Symbiogenesis**: a grafter clinging to a producer may take it in as it divides; its daughter founds a new species
   with the producer's photosynthesis and cell type. The tree of life gains a second parent now and then.
3. **Telling it**: the mind view (*Living off X*, *Clinging to X*, *Carrying grafters*, a lamp), the genome tab and
   card, the chronicler (parasite, partnership and giver stories; *A new kind of life* for symbiogenesis), a GRAFT
   annotation up close, the registry's origin line (*taking in X*).
4. **Stains** (docs/stains.md): Settings → Overlays → Stain, or J. Diet, Lineage, Grafts, Warmth, Motion, Novelty.
5. **A chronicler bug, fixed**: stories meant to be told once (`keyGap: Infinity`: every "Never before in this world"
   first, diet, spark, succession, elder) could never be told, since `t - (-1e9) >= Infinity` is false. They now can;
   the test fixture's founders were counted as sparks once they could, and are fixed too.

## The runs

All at 32k particles, 15 simulated minutes, Dawn on the RTX 2080 SUPER, one run at a time (the machine's memory
allows no more; dynamo was unreachable, so no farm). `off` is grafting disabled (`graftMin` 2) on the same code,
so founding is identical. Late-window means (`tools/night/table.mjs`):

| pool 0.2, take 0.03 | 4 | 98.4 | 27.3 | 0.143 | 11% | 15% |

Frame time is unchanged (2.15 ms a frame with and without). The lesson: grafting is a very strong strategy, and the
pooling term matters more than the grafter's pull. Even "weak" pooling (0.1/s of the difference) moves about 0.15
energy a second from a rich producer to a poor grafter, five times a cell's upkeep: parasitism either way. The flows
have to be on the scale of upkeep.

### The gate, stopped at 14 seeds

The candidate (pool 0.05, take 0.02, one graft per host, `graftRoom` 2, `engulf` 0.002) against
`balance/baseline-32k-30m.json`, 32k, 30 minutes (`runs/night/gate1`). After 14 seeds: 105.6 effective species
against the baseline's 117.2 on the same seeds (−10%), Hill 2 31.8 against 40.3, guilds 3.43 against 3.36,
every run persisted with a food web. About 15–20% of cells are grafted late in a 30-minute world (the share
climbs through the run). I stopped it there to try specialist grafters (a stricter fit with a harder drain:
parasites that track one host, which in real ecology keep the commonest host in check).

### In the page (`tools/night/pagecheck.mjs`, `runs/night/page1`)

No page errors. Every stain cycles with J and shows in the map bar. A picked grafter's last decision read
*Living off a host*, its host named as the target, "+0.065/s along its grafts · −0.064/s net besides": a
parasite whose drain just pays its upkeep. The Vigor stain showed nearly every cell white (it compared energy with
`reproE`, far below the division mark); fixed to use `divE`.

### The next morning

- **Specialists** (fit 0.5–0.6, drains 0.05–0.1) did not help: 97–112 effective species on seeds 1–4 against the
  candidate's 117 and `off`'s 152; the harder the drain, the worse.
- **A graft is no body**: `bonds` counted a graft as a body bond, so a grafter in a crowd of its own kind escaped the
  crowding cost (`kinCost`, the simulation's own Janzen–Connell regulator) and earned body thrift. Fixed (only bonds to
  a cell's own kind count). It is right on its own terms but did not move the numbers: 118.9 over 8 seeds against
  `off`'s 151.5.
- **The chronicler fix** does not flood the log (`tools/chronicle.mjs`, 32k, 90 minutes at Max: 2 stories, the budget
  and the 30 s real-time gap ruling as before); firsts and graft stories now compete with the rest.
- Unit tests: 241 pass.

## Where it stands, and what I'd decide

Every grafting configuration screened costs 10–20% of effective species and Hill 2 (the partial 30-minute gate:
−10% and −21% at 14 seeds), while persistence, food webs and guilds are unchanged. Below the first, far too strong
setting, the configurations differ by less than four or eight seeds can resolve. Grafting species do well (12–25% of
the living belong to them), and a pool where a few successful lineages carry passengers is, by Hill numbers, a less
even one.

Two honest options:

1. Keep grafts on, accepting the diversity indices' cost for a new kind of relationship (parasites, passengers,
   lines born of a union) the world did not have. Run the full farm gate first; it may or may not flag
   `lateEffSpecies`.
2. Ship the rule off by default (`graftMin` 2 in `DEFAULT_K`, or `graftFound` 0 with mutation's gain off) and offer
   it as a world option, like the pool's events.

My recommendation is 2 until a farm gate with dynamo can measure it properly; everything else from the night
(stains, the narrator, the book, the chronicler fix, the kin-only bonds) is independent of it and safe to take.
